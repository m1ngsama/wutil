# Product

## Register

product

## Users

wutil serves the general public and working web users who arrive from search, bookmarks, or shared links because they need one quick utility: merge PDFs, format JSON, generate a password, convert an image, count words, encode text, or calculate a date. They are often in the middle of another task and want the page to be obvious, fast, private, and low-friction.

## Product Purpose

wutil is a collection of lightweight browser-only web tools. Its purpose is to help people complete common utility tasks without accounts, uploads, dashboards, or configuration. Success means a user can land directly on a tool page, understand the task immediately, complete it safely in the browser, and leave with confidence that their input was not sent to a wutil server.

## Brand Personality

Warm, direct, and lively. The product should feel confident without being technical-intimidating, friendly without becoming childish, and polished without adding ceremony. It should feel like a well-designed reference book that is easy to use: clear names, useful affordances, and no wasted explanation.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS blue and gray.
- Glassmorphism, decorative blur, glow effects, and novelty gradients.
- Cyan-on-dark developer-tool styling.
- shadcn/ui defaults without adaptation.
- Dense dashboards, account funnels, and marketing pages that delay the actual tool.
- Repeated identical icon-card grids that look generated rather than considered.

## Design Principles

1. Typography leads. Every tool page has a strong masthead and an obvious job name.
2. Get out of the way. People arrive with a task, so the first screen should expose the working tool rather than a pitch.
3. Privacy is a product behavior. File and text workflows should remain local-first and explain data handling only where it helps trust.
4. Warmth over neutrality. The interface can feel editorial and humane while still being restrained.
5. Scan first, read second. Labels, controls, examples, and states should make the next action clear without tutorial copy.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Treat WCAG A/AA as the baseline for public pages and tool workflows. Maintain keyboard access for controls, visible focus states, high contrast in both light and dark themes, reduced motion friendliness, responsive layouts without horizontal overflow, and clear error states that do not rely on color alone.
